Expert 3 - Elena Navarro
Role: Head of Sustainable Mobility, City Transit Agency
Market: Spain

00:00
Interviewer: How would you describe adoption in Spain?

00:16
Elena Navarro: Adoption is increasing, and in some larger city agencies electric buses are becoming the default for new orders. Access still varies a lot between cities.

01:02
Interviewer: What are the main barriers?

01:09
Elena Navarro: Funding matters, but I would say depot and workshop readiness is just as important. You can order vehicles, but if the depot cannot charge and service them the rollout stalls.

02:04
Interviewer: How important is total cost of ownership?

02:12
Elena Navarro: It matters, but the discussion is not purely financial. Agencies also weigh air quality commitments, passenger experience and whether the fleet helps them win the next concession.

03:05
Interviewer: So would you say economics are less important in Spain?

03:13
Elena Navarro: I would say economics and policy commitments are balanced. I would not say the financial case alone decides the purchase.

04:02
Interviewer: What is your outlook for the next three to five years?

04:11
Elena Navarro: I am quite positive. Adoption could accelerate if depot funding expands and vehicle prices keep falling. I could see fleet growth above 15 percent annually in some regions.

05:04
Interviewer: What about purchase timelines?

05:12
Elena Navarro: Around six to nine months can happen when the funding is already allocated. If the agency has to wait for a new budget cycle it takes considerably longer.

06:03
Interviewer: Any final thought?

06:10
Elena Navarro: The main point is that adoption is not just about buying vehicles. Agencies need trained drivers, ready depots and enough daily kilometres for the programme to hold together.
