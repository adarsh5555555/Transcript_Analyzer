Expert 2 - Marek Kowalski
Role: Former Procurement Director, Municipal Transport Authority
Market: Poland

00:00
Interviewer: How would you describe electric bus adoption in Poland today?

00:18
Marek Kowalski: It is growing but very uneven. A few large cities have committed fully, while many mid-sized municipalities are still running pilot routes.

01:06
Interviewer: What are the main barriers?

01:13
Marek Kowalski: Purchase price is the first barrier. These are large capital items and municipal budgets are tight. The second issue is proving the buses will be used enough to justify the cost.

02:05
Interviewer: What does procurement actually look at?

02:12
Marek Kowalski: We look at total cost of ownership, expected daily kilometres, battery warranty, service contracts and training requirements. Environmental commitments help, but the financial case decides whether it gets approved.

03:04
Interviewer: How important is driver training?

03:11
Marek Kowalski: Very important operationally. If the depot buys vehicles but only a few drivers are comfortable with them, utilisation drops and the business case weakens.

04:06
Interviewer: Do you expect adoption to accelerate?

04:14
Marek Kowalski: Yes, but I would not expect a dramatic jump. Growth will be gradual, because cities have competing capital priorities such as track renewal.

05:03
Interviewer: Any rough expectation for the next three to five years?

05:11
Marek Kowalski: I would expect continued growth, but closer to high single digits or low double digits across the whole national fleet, rather than anything like 25 percent.

06:02
Interviewer: How long can the purchase process take?

06:09
Marek Kowalski: Twelve to eighteen months is common. Procurement, the operator, finance and the city council all have to align, so it moves slowly.
